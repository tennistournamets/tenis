Sixteen players is the classic tournament size: the bracket is symmetrical, no byes are needed and the event fits into one day. Here is how to build it.

## Rounds and matches

A 16-player single elimination bracket has **4 rounds and 15 matches**:

| Round | Matches | Players left |
|---|---|---|
| Round of 16 | 8 | 8 |
| Quarter-finals | 4 | 4 |
| Semi-finals | 2 | 2 |
| Final | 1 | 1 |

The rule is simple: there is always one match fewer than players, because each match knocks one player out.

## Seeding

If you know the players' level, place the strongest so they do not meet too early. The standard first-round pairs are **1–16, 8–9, 5–12, 4–13, 3–14, 6–11, 7–10, 2–15**. Seeds 1 and 2 end up in opposite halves and can only meet in the final. Everyone else is drawn at random.

## Fewer than 16 players

If, say, 12 players sign up, the bracket is still built for 16 with 4 empty places. The top four seeds skip the first round (a bye) and go straight to the quarter-finals.

## How long it takes

Assume a match takes about an hour and you have 4 courts:

- round of 16 — 8 matches, 2 waves on 4 courts: ~2 h;
- quarter-finals — 4 matches: ~1 h;
- semi-finals and the final — ~1 h each.

That is about **5 hours** plus breaks. With 2 courts, plan for about 8 hours.

## Other formats for 16 players

- **Double elimination** — everyone gets a second chance in the losers' bracket. There will be about 30 matches, but nobody goes home after one bad match.
- **4 groups of 4 + playoff** — everyone plays at least 3 group matches (24 in total), then the top two of each group go to the quarter-finals (7 more matches).
- **Round robin** — 16 players means 120 matches, so it only suits a long league.
